❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What image formats does this converter accept?

The tool exclusively processes PNG, JPEG, and WebP image files up to 10 megabytes in size. These formats provide the optimal balance of quality and compression for accurate character recognition. Other formats like BMP, TIFF, or GIF are intentionally excluded to maintain processing speed and security standards.

Is my uploaded image stored permanently on your servers?

No. Your image is transmitted securely over HTTPS solely for the duration of the OCR processing. Once the text is extracted and returned to your browser, the file is not retained, archived, or shared. All temporary processing data is discarded immediately by the recognition engine.

Why did the tool fail to extract text from my image?

Extraction failures typically occur when the source image has extremely low resolution, severe motion blur, heavy shadowing, or stylized decorative fonts that deviate significantly from standard typography. Try capturing a clearer photo with better lighting and higher contrast between the text and its background.

Can I extract text from mobile phone screenshots?

Absolutely. Screenshots are among the best candidates for OCR because they contain digitally rendered text with perfect sharpness and high contrast. App interfaces, chat histories, error messages, and web page captures all yield near-perfect extraction accuracy.

Does the extracted text retain original formatting like bold or italics?

The OCR engine extracts plain Unicode text. While it preserves line breaks and paragraph spacing to maintain readability, stylistic formatting such as bold, italics, font sizes, or colors is not carried over. The output is designed for content extraction rather than visual replication.

Is there a limit to how much text can be extracted at once?

The limitation is tied to the image file size rather than the text volume. As long as your image remains under the 10MB threshold and the text is legible, the engine will process everything visible in the frame, whether it is a single sentence or a densely packed full page.
