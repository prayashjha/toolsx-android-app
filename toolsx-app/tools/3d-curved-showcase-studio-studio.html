🎯 How It Works

1

Upload Your Design

Drag & drop an SVG, PNG, JPG, or WebP file into the studio. SVG files with filled paths become clean extruded solids, while photos and raster images become detailed height-map reliefs.

2

Customize Settings

Adjust extrusion depth, bevel thickness, curve smoothness, relief height, and base thickness. Every change rebuilds the model instantly so you can dial in the exact look you want.

3

Preview in Real 3D

Orbit, zoom, and pan the WebGL viewport with your mouse or finger. The turntable spins by default so you instantly see true depth — tap or drag to take manual control at any time.

4

Export & Print

Choose binary or ASCII STL, set the longest side in millimetres, and download a print-ready file. GLB export is also available when you need colours and textures for rendering.

✨ Features

  • SVG Path Extrusion: Filled vector paths become watertight 3D solids with optional rounded bevel edges
  • Image-to-Relief: Photos convert into lithophane-style height maps with adjustable smoothing and inversion
  • Real-Time WebGL Preview: Studio lighting, soft shadows, and physically-based materials render instantly in the browser
  • Dual STL Export: Binary for compact slicer-friendly files, ASCII for human-readable debugging
  • 100% Private: Every file is processed locally on your device — nothing is uploaded to any server
  • Print-Ready Scaling: Models auto-normalise and export at your exact millimetre dimensions for Cura, PrusaSlicer, and Bambu Studio

🎨 Use Cases

Makers use this converter to turn brand logos into desk plaques, photographers turn portraits into lithophane lamps, and engineers turn technical outlines into functional brackets. Jewellery designers extrude ornamental vectors into castable masters, while teachers convert classroom artwork into tactile prints. Because everything runs client-side, studios with strict confidentiality rules can safely prototype licensed artwork without a single byte leaving the machine.

💡 Tips for Best Results

  • Use SVGs with closed, filled paths — stroke-only lines cannot be extruded
  • High-contrast photos produce the crispest relief detail
  • Default curve smoothness of 24 is ideal; raise to 32 only for tiny curved lettering
  • Keep bevel enabled at the default 0.10 / 0.08 for strong, smooth printed edges
  • Leave base thickness around 0.25 so reliefs stand upright on the print bed
  • Export between 80 mm and 150 mm for most desktop FDM printers
