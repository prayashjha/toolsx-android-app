How Does This Calculator Work?

1. What is EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)?

EMI is the fixed amount you pay each month toward your loan. Each EMI contains two parts: Principal (the loan amount) and Interest (the cost of borrowing). In the beginning, interest makes up most of the EMI, and over time, the principal portion grows larger.

2. How Down Payment Affects Your EMI

Down payment is the amount you pay upfront from your own savings. A larger down payment means you borrow less, which directly reduces both your monthly EMI and the total interest paid over the loan tenure.

3. Reducing Balance Method Explained

This method calculates interest only on the remaining outstanding balance. As you pay down the principal each month, the interest portion of your next EMI automatically decreases.

Common EMI Mistakes to Avoid

1. Only Focusing on Monthly EMI

Many borrowers focus only on the monthly EMI. But on a 30-year loan, you could pay 2-3 times the original amount in interest. Always check the total interest payable before committing.

2. Ignoring Processing Fees

Banks charge 0.5% to 2% as processing fees, which can add up to thousands or lakhs depending on your loan size. Include these in your total cost calculation.

3. Floating vs Fixed Rate Confusion

Fixed rates stay constant throughout the tenure, while floating rates change with market conditions. Floating rates may start lower but can rise later.

4. Overlooking Prepayment Penalties

Some banks charge prepayment penalties on fixed-rate loans. Read the terms carefully. RBI guidelines state no prepayment penalty on floating rate loans.

Pro Tip: How to Reduce Your EMI Burden

Use Part-Prepayment Wisely

If you receive a bonus or windfall, prepay directly toward the principal instead of paying extra EMI. This reduces your outstanding balance faster and can save you lakhs in total interest without increasing your monthly EMI.
